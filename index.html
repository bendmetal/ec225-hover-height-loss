Source: RFM Section SUP.11.1, Figure 8b (2 of 2) (MAKILA 2A1), rev 15-40 — Height Loss after Engine Failure in Hover Flight, with wind effect, MPAI bullets closed. The same chart appears in the SAR and dual-hoist supplements (SUP.5.1 Figure 4b, SUP.25.1 Figure 8b). Digitized 2026-11-23 from the scan embedded in the RFM, all three panels, and checked against both of the chart’s printed examples to within 0.1 ft.

Weight Index comes from RFM Section 5.1.A1, Figure 9 (MAKILA 2A1), rev 11-51 — the version for aircraft without mod 07.26632, floor Hσ = −2,000 ft. SUP.11.1 reprints that exact chart as Figure 8b (1 of 2) on its replacement page “RC a”. It is an exact standard-atmosphere relation, WI = weight ÷ 1,000 ÷ σ, computed that way here.

How the chart is read here. Each colour has eight altitude curves, −2,000 to +5,000 ft. Where the curves merge into one band and cannot be told apart, the band’s bottom edge is used — always the larger height loss, by at most 6 ft (Normal) or 10 ft (Training). Each curve stops where the chart stops it, at about ISA +35. The chart’s own printed examples are colder than this aircraft may fly (below −2,000 ft density altitude), so this tool refuses them. It never guesses or extrapolates.
